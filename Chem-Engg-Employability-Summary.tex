\begin{frame}{1. Problem Identification \& Root Cause Analysis}
  \scriptsize
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{L{2.2cm} L{3.8cm} L{4.2cm} L{3.5cm}}
    \toprule
    \rowcolor{darkred!10}
    \textbf{Issue Area} & \textbf{Current Bottleneck \& Perception} & \textbf{Task Force Diagnosis \& Reality} & \textbf{Strategic Objective} \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Talent Migration} & Top B.Tech students actively choose careers outside traditional core sectors (finance, consulting, generic software). & Students perceive core chemical engineering as having lower compensation and slower career growth. & Make core Chemical Engineering an employer of choice with upwardly mobile careers. \\
    \addlinespace[3pt]
    \textbf{Market Equilibrium} & Self-reinforcing sub-optimal loop: Low student interest $\rightarrow$ Fewer top recruits $\rightarrow$ Less corporate incentive to offer high-value roles. & Industry misapplies IIT talent as entry-level plant/production engineers rather than high-end technological problem solvers. & Break the cycle from both ends: elevate student capability and co-create premium roles with leadership. \\
    \addlinespace[3pt]
    \textbf{Ecosystem Misconception} & Scope is narrowly associated with refineries, petrochemicals, bulk chemicals, and shop-floor operations. & The ecosystem is expanding into semiconductors, battery tech, biopharma, AI/ML, dynamic modeling, and decarbonization. & Shift focus from narrow plant operations to a broad technology platform. \\
    \addlinespace[3pt]
    \textbf{Intervention Strategy} & Traditional focus relies on passive exposure (arranging plant visits, guest talks, recruitment drives). & Exposure alone fails to shift career choices without building core technical capability and tangible opportunities. & Focus heavily on \textbf{Capability} (curriculum/skills) and \textbf{Opportunity} (PPOs/Practice School). \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{frame}
